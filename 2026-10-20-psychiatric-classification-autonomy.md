## Essential distinctions

1. Observation: what a person reports or an assessor observes.
2. Classification: rules mapping observations to a named category.
3. Explanation: evidence identifying mechanisms or causes.
4. Prognosis: independently validated predictions.
5. Treatment utility: whether using the classification improves outcomes.
6. Authority: justification for any restriction or requirement.

Evidence at one level does not automatically validate the next. Reliability, agreement with an interview standard, biological explanation, treatment benefit, and authority to override choices are different propositions.

A descriptive label used to explain the observations defining that label risks circular reasoning. Absence of a single biomarker does not by itself disprove a syndrome; presence of a diagnostic label does not establish a mechanism.

### E01 — Hamilton Depression Rating Scale: item content and scoring

Instrument copies:
- University of Florida-hosted form: https://dcf.psychiatry.ufl.edu/files/2011/05/HAMILTON-DEPRESSION.pdf
- American Psychological Association-hosted adaptation: https://www.apa.org/depression-guideline/hamilton-rating-scale.pdf

Observed: the 17-item total combines mood, guilt, suicidality, three insomnia items, work/activity, slowing, agitation, psychological and bodily anxiety, gastrointestinal complaints, bodily pain/fatigue, sexual symptoms, health preoccupation, weight loss, and insight.

In the UF-hosted copy, insight scoring gives zero for accepting depression/illness, a higher score for accepting illness while attributing it to circumstances or physical explanations such as overwork or a virus, and a still higher score for denying illness. Its health-preoccupation item includes frequent complaints and requests for help. The APA-hosted copy uses different insight anchors and explicitly references the patient's understanding/background.

Analysis: these anchors create a risk that disagreement, plausible alternative causal accounts, or justified help-seeking increase apparent severity. Actual bias in a trial must be demonstrated from its version, interview guide, ratings, and procedures; it is not established for every Hamilton study.

The APA adaptation allocates up to six combined points to three insomnia items and four to mood. Totals therefore embed weighting choices. Different profiles can yield identical totals; improvements and deterioration can offset each other. A sleep-driven reduction could occur without improvement in the person's central difficulty: this is a measurement threat to investigate, not a finding about every intervention.

The UF commentary identifies inpatient origins and omission of excessive sleep/eating from this version. Do not attribute the scoring anchors of one adaptation to all versions, or claim an agency uses Hamilton without evidence.

### E02 — PHQ-9 original validation

Kroenke, Spitzer, Williams (2001), The PHQ-9: Validity of a Brief Depression Severity Measure.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1495268/
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11556941/
https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1525-1497.2001.016009606.x

Primary study: validation included an independent blinded professional interview in 580 patients. Cutoff at least 10: reported sensitivity 88%, specificity 88%.

Items cover pleasure/interest, depressed/hopeless feelings, sleep, fatigue, appetite, self-worth, concentration, slowing/restlessness, and death/self-harm thoughts. Frequency over two weeks is scored.

Analysis: comparison with a structured clinical interview supports agreement with that reference classification. It is not independent proof of an underlying disease mechanism or need for a particular treatment. Functional associations provide additional evidence but do not alone establish psychiatric causation. Sensitivity/specificity are not the probability that an individual positive screen is correct; predictive value depends on prevalence and setting. Do not describe the instrument as having no validation.

### E03 — Heterogeneity within one depression diagnosis

Fried and Nesse (2015), Depression is not a consistent syndrome: an investigation of unique symptom patterns in the STAR*D study.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4397113/
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2014.10.010

Primary analysis: 3,703 outpatients, 1,030 distinct symptom profiles; most frequent profile 1.8%. Symptoms were dichotomized, and medical conditions/medications could affect presentation.

Analysis: strong evidence against casually assuming a uniform presentation or interchangeable symptoms. These are operationalized profiles, not 1,030 validated disease subtypes. Heterogeneity alone does not logically invalidate every possible use of the category.

Related review, distinct from primary research:
Fried and Nesse (2015), Depression sum-scores don't add up.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4386095/
https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-015-0325-4

### E04 — Institutional acknowledgment of classification limits

NIMH (2024), Revolutionizing the Study of Mental Disorders.
https://www.nimh.nih.gov/news/science-updates/2024/revolutionizing-the-study-of-mental-disorders

Official account recognizes symptom overlap, all-or-none cutoffs, and poor alignment between existing categories and causal information. It also describes clinical utility. This is an institutional acknowledgment, not independent validation of either the whole diagnostic system or its wholesale rejection. RDoC is a research framework, not an established clinical replacement.

### E05 — Antidepressant symptom-score effects

Stone et al. (2022), BMJ, individual-participant analysis of FDA-submitted trials.
https://www.bmj.com/content/378/bmj-2021-067606

232 trials; 73,388 participants; average drug-placebo difference approximately 1.75 Hamilton-17-equivalent points. A mixture model estimated a substantial additional effect in roughly 15%.

Interpretation: an average scale contrast and model-based effect distribution do not establish durable net benefit. The 15% estimate must not be inverted into a claim that 85% received no help. Verify scale conversions, model assumptions, follow-up, exclusions, and item-level changes.

### E06 — Psychotherapy response and remission

Cuijpers et al. (2021).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34107050/

228-trial synthesis: approximately 41% response after psychotherapy versus 17% usual care and 16% waiting list around two months; remission approximately one third. Response generally means at least 50% symptom reduction. Some outcomes were estimated; study quality and control type matter.

Nonresponse under a threshold is not no improvement. Response is not necessarily durable recovery or benefit exceeding harms. Examine comparator quality, assessor masking, attrition, estimated versus directly reported outcomes, and meaningful functioning chosen by patients.

### E07 — STAR*D reporting and denominator dispute

Pigott et al. (2023) protocol-based reanalysis.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10373710/

Reanalysis reports approximately 35% cumulative remission under its protocol-based approach, versus the widely cited original 67%; alternative supplemental measurement yields another estimate. Investigate eligibility, denominators, outcome instrument, missingness, attrition, and cumulative probability assumptions.

Open-label sequential treatment without a placebo group cannot independently identify medication-specific causal benefit. Nonremission does not equal absence of improvement.

### E08 — Publication bias

Turner et al. (2008).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18199864/

Published literature made 94% of trials appear positive versus 51% in FDA assessments. These percentages concern trials, not patients.

Turner et al. (2022) update.
https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1003886

Thirty trials, 13,747 participants; all 15 positive trials transparently published, versus seven of 15 negative trials; six negative trials unpublished and two misreported. FDA-based versus journal-based effect sizes approximately 0.24 versus 0.29. Bias persisted despite improvement from the earlier cohort.

Supports scrutiny of evidence production and reporting incentives; does not itself prove deliberate universal patient entrapment.

### E09 — Antipsychotic discontinuation and competing explanations

CATIE (2005).
https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa051688

About 74% discontinued assigned medication within 18 months. Discontinuation had multiple reasons, including efficacy and tolerability. It cannot be equated with 74% receiving no benefit. Examine metabolic harms, patient preference, switching, and reasons for stopping separately.

### E10 — Coercion outcomes and rights

OCTET (2013).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23537605/

333 participants in England; substantially longer compulsory supervision did not reduce the reported 12-month readmission rate, approximately 36% in each arm. Specific setting/intervention: not a trial of all care or a finding about a US benefits agency.

WHO/OHCHR guidance (2023).
https://www.who.int/news/item/09-10-2023-who-ohchr-launch-new-guidance-to-improve-laws-addressing-human-rights-abuses-in-mental-health-care

Official human-rights guidance addresses coercion and free informed consent. Normative guidance is not binding New Jersey law or direct proof of a specific agency violation.

### E11 — Medication dependence and withdrawal

FDA benzodiazepine boxed-warning update.
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requiring-boxed-warning-updated-improve-safe-use-benzodiazepine-drug-class

Official warnings recognize dependence and withdrawal risks, including prescribed use. Physical dependence and addiction must not be conflated.

Henssler et al. (2024).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38851198/

Synthesis reports discontinuation symptoms around 31% after antidepressants versus 17% after placebo; severe symptoms around 2.8% versus 0.6%. Exposure duration, ascertainment, heterogeneity, and long-term generalizability require examination.

Kalfas et al. (2025).
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632531/

A standardized symptom-score difference around 0.31 is not a 31% incidence estimate. Compare instruments, duration, tapering, and withdrawal/relapse adjudication before reconciling estimates.

### E12 — Findings that challenge a universal failure claim

ANTLER (2021).
https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2106356

Among selected established antidepressant users, relapse occurred in approximately 39% continuing versus 56% discontinuing over 52 weeks. Selection of prior users, withdrawal, masking, and relapse definitions constrain interpretation. Preserve this result and interrogate it rather than omitting inconvenient evidence.

Leucht et al. (2012), antipsychotic relapse prevention synthesis.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22560607/

Reported relapse around 27% maintenance versus 64% placebo at one year. Independently recheck the exact population, discontinuation design, harms, and applicability before reuse. Relapse-prevention estimates in selected populations cannot settle the net-benefit question across psychiatry.

## Research program

### A. Audit the diagnostic inference chain

For each category separately, extract:
- Exact manual edition, criteria, exclusions, interview, scale version, and scoring anchors.
- Origin and empirical justification of symptom selection, weights, duration, and thresholds.
- Assessor agreement, test-retest stability, and setting-specific uncertainty.
- Whether the reference standard is independent of the construct being tested.
- Evidence of discriminant, predictive, causal, and treatment-selection validity.
- Alternative physical, environmental, economic, cultural, medication-related, and interpersonal explanations.
- Whether counterevidence can genuinely revise or remove the classification.
- Whether dissent, refusal, complaints, or unconventional goals enter the score.

Do not generalize depression findings to every diagnosis without category-specific evidence.

### B. Audit functional impairment

Require operational answers:
1. Which activity is difficult, according to whom, and desired by whom?
2. What directly demonstrates inability rather than choice, disagreement, exclusion, or missing resources?
3. Which employment/productivity assumptions are built into the instrument?
4. Are limitations attributed to psychiatric causes after independent investigation?
5. Does the measure reflect the person's own goals and context?
6. What alternative explanation would change the assessor's conclusion?
7. Can the person contest the assessment without that challenge increasing apparent pathology?

Research socioeconomic and cultural measurement bias, especially where conventional employment is used as a proxy for health. A critique of imposed norms does not require denying self-reported limitations or withholding requested assistance.

### C. Reassess efficacy without importing the classification's assumptions

Define helped before aggregating:
- Specific experience or activity improved.
- Magnitude meaningful to the patient.
- Duration and durability.
- Incremental effect versus credible controls.
- Harms, withdrawal, burden, and opportunity costs.
- Patient autonomy, quality of life, and ability to pursue chosen goals.

Report raw item trajectories and opposing changes alongside total scores. Separate response, remission, discontinuation, relapse, functioning, and net benefit. Investigate compromised blinding, selective publication, attrition, enrichment, missing-data assumptions, and short follow-up.

For the majority hypothesis, prespecify population, treatment classes, denominator, duration, comparator, meaningful-benefit threshold, and harm weighting. Trial counts are not patient counts; heterogeneous percentages cannot be averaged into an industry-wide failure rate.

### D. Test the institutional feedback hypothesis

Trace evidence for each proposed link:
classification -> treatment/referral requirement -> revenue or performance incentive -> adverse outcome/dependence -> reinterpretation as illness -> extended institutional involvement.

Collect actual contracts, reimbursement rules, performance measures, policies, longitudinal outcomes, complaints, appeals, and classification reversals. Distinguish incentive structures, observed behavior, causal effects, and claims of deliberate intent.

### E. Benefits-linked assessment and consent

General policy inquiry only. Obtain current written rules, assessment tools, referral criteria, alternative activities, sanction procedures, appeal protections, consent forms, data-sharing limits, and outcome audits.
