# Temporal State Resolution

## Thesis

Computational acceleration can progressively collapse **epistemic latency** between formulation, prediction, verification, and action. Parallel, probabilistic, analog, quantum, and physically embedded computation can replace portions of serial search by exploiting structure in a problem or physical state space.

This does not establish that physical time, dynamical evolution, or processuality disappears. The defensible claim is that the experienced epistemic structure of temporal unfolding is partly determined by the computational latency and predictive limitations of the observer.

## Core Mechanics

### 1. Duration as an Epistemic Artifact

A bounded observer experiences temporal duration partly because it cannot resolve complex state transitions immediately. Computation therefore introduces a latency between a question and its predicted or verified consequence.

This latency is **observer-relative computational duration**, not physical duration. Faster computation changes access to evolving physical states; it does not establish that the underlying dynamics cease to evolve.

### 2. Serial Search → State-Space Resolution

Conventional problem solving often traverses a sequence of hypotheses, experiments, failures, iterations, and implementations. Advanced computational architectures can replace portions of this traversal with parallel search, optimization, simulation, inference, or physical relaxation.

The relevant distinction is:

* **Algorithmic search complexity:** computational work required by an algorithm.
* **Physical relaxation time:** time required for the substrate to evolve toward a state.
* **Epistemic resolution time:** time required for an observer to obtain sufficient information to determine or act upon a state.

These quantities can diverge substantially. A computation may reduce epistemic resolution time by orders of magnitude without eliminating physical evolution.

### 3. Physical State Resolution

A physical system can encode computational problems directly into its dynamics. Probabilistic and analog architectures can exploit stochastic transitions, energy landscapes, attractors, or other substrate-level properties rather than executing every logical operation through conventional sequential instructions.

This constitutes a genuine transition from exclusively instruction-sequential computation toward **physically embedded state resolution**.

It does not imply instantaneous computation.

### 4. Prediction and the Future

A sufficiently capable predictive system can represent a large set of possible future states before those states physically occur. Prediction can therefore reduce uncertainty about future configurations without making those configurations physically instantiated.

The following relations must remain distinct:

**computability ≠ prediction ≠ representation ≠ physical realization**

A represented future state is not thereby an already existing physical state.

### 5. Limits

Physical computation remains constrained by:

* dynamical evolution;
* information propagation;
* energy;
* information capacity;
* noise;
* error correction;
* thermodynamics;
* quantum mechanics;
* quantum speed limits;
* relativistic signaling constraints.

Quantum speed-limit theory establishes nonzero lower bounds on state evolution under specified physical conditions, depending on physical quantities such as energy variance and mean energy. [Nature Communications — Observation of minimal and maximal speed limits for few and many-body states](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56451-3)

Lloyd's physical limits likewise establish finite computational capacity constrained by physical resources rather than convergence of computation time toward zero. [Nature — Ultimate Physical Limits to Computation](https://www.nature.com/articles/35023282)

### 6. Landauer Correction

Landauer's principle states an energetic lower bound for logically irreversible information erasure:

$$
E_{\min}=k_BT\ln 2
$$

This is an **energy bound**, not a computational-latency bound. It therefore does not justify:

$$
\tau_{\text{Landauer}}\approx0
$$

Finite-time thermodynamics instead establishes additional energetic costs associated with performing erasure within finite operational times; reducing operational time can increase the required dissipation. [PubMed — Landauer principle and thermodynamics of computation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40345217/) [Physical Review Letters — Finite-Time Landauer Principle](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32955336/)

Quantum formulations likewise address finite-time thermodynamic constraints rather than establishing instantaneous erasure. [Physical Review Letters — Finite-Time Quantum Landauer Principle and Quantum Coherence](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.010602)

### 7. Probabilistic Computation

Probabilistic hardware demonstrates that computation can emerge through physical stochastic dynamics rather than conventional deterministic instruction sequences. Borders et al. demonstrated integer factorization using stochastic magnetic tunnel junctions, including factorization up to 945 using an asynchronous probabilistic network. [Nature — Integer factorization using stochastic magnetic tunnel junctions](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1557-9)

The result demonstrates physically embedded probabilistic computation, not instantaneous solution or elimination of time.

### 8. McTaggart and Temporal Ordering

McTaggart's C-series should be treated as a philosophical formal ordering, not a computational architecture or demonstrated machine state space.

The conceptual analogy may be useful: an ordered set of states can be considered independently of the observer's sequential experience of them. However, equating the C-series directly with computational "topological constraint resolution" introduces an unsupported conceptual substitution.

[The Unreality of Time](https://www.logicmuseum.com/time/mctaggartunreality.htm)
[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — John McTaggart](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2020/entries/mctaggart/)

### 9. Ashby's Law

Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety establishes a requirement concerning a controller's capacity to regulate disturbances. It does not establish:

$$
V_{\text{Machine}}\equiv V_{\text{Substrate}}
$$

Nor does sufficient control variety imply zero entropy or zero observational latency.

The appropriate interpretation is that computational architecture must possess sufficient relevant variety to regulate the disturbances and state relationships within its control problem.

## Revised Central Proposition

> **Computational acceleration progressively collapses epistemic latency between formulation, prediction, verification, and action while leaving physical dynamical evolution intact.**

In the asymptotic computational limit, an observer may represent an increasingly large portion of a relevant state space without physically sampling every candidate state sequentially. The resulting reduction in experienced temporal separation can become extremely large.

The correct limiting concept is therefore not:

$$
\tau\rightarrow0
$$

but:

$$
\tau_{\text{epistemic}}\ll\tau_{\text{serial-search}}
$$

subject to the physical constraints of the computational substrate.

## System Model

$$
\text{physical state}
\rightarrow
\text{measurement}
\rightarrow
\text{computation}
\rightarrow
\text{prediction}
\rightarrow
\text{verification}
\rightarrow
\text{action}
$$

Optimization compresses the computational intervals within this chain. It does not necessarily compress the physical evolution of the underlying system.

Thus:

$$
\text{computational acceleration}
\neq
\text{physical temporal annihilation}
$$

and:

$$
\text{epistemic latency reduction}
\rightarrow
\text{apparently collapsed temporal structure}
$$

## Terminology

Replace **"collapse of processual time"** with **"collapse of observer-relative computational latency."**

Replace:

> "Time is an artifact of bounded computation."

with:

> "The experienced epistemic structure of temporal unfolding is partly determined by the computational latency and predictive limitations of the observer."

This formulation preserves the original computational insight while removing unsupported ontological claims.

## Conceptual Synthesis

Human cognition ordinarily experiences reality through sequentially resolved information. Advanced computational systems can construct, compare, predict, and verify many relationships without requiring each possibility to be physically traversed as an independent sequential experiment.

The resulting phenomenon is best described as **temporal-state compression at the epistemic level**: the observer requires less computational time to resolve relationships that remain physically distributed across evolving states.

**Serial time is not eliminated; serial epistemic access is compressed.**
